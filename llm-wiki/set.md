# Set

Set is the archive's best case study in why an Egyptian god cannot be reduced to a moral label. He is murderer and royal rival, desert storm and foreign power, sexually aggressive challenger and necessary defender of Re. His history is not a steady unveiling of an “evil god,” but a changing placement of dangerous force.

## Character and function

Set belongs to the Ennead as brother of Osiris, Isis, and Nephthys. He disrupts succession by killing Osiris and contests Horus's right to rule. His landscapes are desert margins and foreign zones; his forces include storms, noise, separation, aggression, and irregularity. In ritual scenes, Sethian animals and enemies can embody what must be subdued.

Yet Set's strength is useful aboard Re's solar bark, where he spears or restrains Apep. Some royal traditions coordinate Horus and Set as the powers of the Two Lands; a “two faces” or “two ba” unity can express totality through joined opposites. Set is internal conflict capable of office; Apep is recurrent cosmic obstruction.

## Iconography

The “Set animal” has a canine-like body, rectangular ears, a long curved snout, and a raised forked tail. No zoological identification is secure. Te Velde's influential analysis treats its strangeness as meaningful, while proposals have ranged across aardvark, donkey, jackal, fish, and composite. Late demonization could replace direct images with damaged or substitute signs.

Animals classed as Sethian vary by ritual context—donkey, hippopotamus, crocodile, antelope, pig, fish, oryx, and others can be made into enemies. This does not mean each species always “is Set.” Classification is situational.

## Places and historical change

Major associations in the student's report include Ombos/Naqada and Sepermeru, a “gateway to the desert.” Set enjoyed royal prestige in the Ramesside period: the names Seti (“man of Set”) and Sethnakht preserve it. The Hyksos identified their favored storm god with Set at Avaris, strengthening his foreign associations without making him immediately taboo.

A late Nineteenth Dynasty stela from Deir el-Medina depicts an official worshipping Set, and a Ramesside amulet gives Set the double crown. These objects are compact proof that murder in myth did not eliminate positive cult or royal legitimacy. See [[web-research-supplement#Set remains double-sided]].

Set's cult and images were attacked in many first-millennium contexts, particularly as foreign domination and Osirian/Horian temple programs reframed him. The student's report plausibly connects demonization to political memory, but its smooth chronology should be treated as a synthesis, not an uncontested single-cause explanation. Local veneration and positive roles persisted unevenly.

## Main texts in the archive

- [[osiris-isis-horus-and-set|Contendings of Horus and Seth]]: succession dispute, humiliations, transformations, judgment.
- Great Hymn and Lamentations: Set is largely displaced by attention to Osiris's restoration.
- Plutarch: late continuous murder narrative with chest and conspirators.
- Pyramid/Coffin Texts and Underworld Books: conflict, bodily injury, reconciliation, and solar defense.
- Demotic and Greek magical texts: late divine names and powers in new ritual combinations.

## The student's report: what to keep and qualify

Keep its central insight: Set's destructive acts can generate or defend a larger order, and his demonization changes historically. Qualify three claims: that he was simply “the villain of the Ennead” at all times, that foreign invasion alone explains his decline, and that “He with Two Faces” has one recoverable esoteric meaning. The drafts themselves increasingly recognize uncertainty.

## On a trip

- At Edfu, read bound or speared Sethian beings as Horus-temple ritual theology, not a neutral portrait of all Set worship.
- At Kom Ombo, remember ancient Ombos/Naqada (Set's Upper Egyptian center) is a different site from the famous Kom Ombo temple of Sobek/Haroeris.
- At Karnak and Ramesside monuments, royal names and battle scenes preserve a period when Set's force could be openly royal.
