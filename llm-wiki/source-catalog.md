# Source catalog

This catalog groups 72 physical files into 36 intellectual-source groups. “Student” means a course artifact and not independent authority. “Primary translation” means a modern translation of an ancient source. Scanned files were OCRed for analysis; OCR text is not part of the permanent wiki and should be checked against page images before quoting. [[reading-audit]] states exactly which groups were read in full, compared version-by-version, or sampled by contents and relevant sections.

## C01 — REL 395 syllabus

**Status:** course administration / authoritative for course structure.  
**Use:** identifies REL 395, instructor, learning outcomes, required books, assessment, and calendar. The user's “REL 491” conflicts with this primary course record.

- [REL 395 Syllabus 2017.docx](<../raw/REL 395 Syllabus 2017.docx>)

## C02 — Pinch, Handbook of Egyptian Mythology

**Status:** scholarly reference, Geraldine Pinch, 2002.  
**Use:** course spine for history/sources, myth theory, time cycles, deities, cult centers, and primary-source orientation. The file contains the full 275-page book. Its complete contents and the syllabus-assigned sections were reviewed; other entries were sampled by course topic.

- [Handbook of Egyptian Mythology.pdf](<../raw/(Handbooks of world mythology) Geraldine Pinch-Handbook of Egyptian mythology-ABC-CLIO (2002).pdf>)

## C03 — Teeter, Religion and Ritual in Ancient Egypt

**Status:** scholarly monograph, Emily Teeter, 2011.  
**Use:** strongest archive source for priests, temples, festivals, personal piety, divine communication, death, ancestors, magic, and Amarna. Its complete contents, introduction/afterword, every chapter boundary, and syllabus-targeted sections were reviewed. Some broad characterizations of an “Egyptian mind” should be read as interpretive generalizations.

- [Religion and Ritual in Ancient Egypt.pdf](<../raw/Emily Teeter-Religion and Ritual in Ancient Egypt-Cambridge University Press (2011).pdf>)

## C11 — PDM and PGM magical texts

## C18 — Plutarch, Isis and Osiris

**Status:** primary Greco-Roman literary source in modern translation.  
**Use:** a late Greek account of Egyptian gods, including the continuous chest/dismemberment narrative. Essential for reception; not a timeless transcript of Pharaonic belief.

- [Plutarch_Isis&Osiris.pdf](<../raw/Set/Plutarch_Isis&Osiris.pdf>)

## C19 — Te Velde, Seth, God of Confusion

**Status:** influential scholarly monograph, H. te Velde, 1967.  
**Use:** Set's name/animal, Horus relationship, Osiris murder, Apep defense, and foreignness. The 190-page scan required full OCR; its contents, framing, chapter conclusions, and all passages supporting the student report were reviewed. Valuable but older; some structuralist/psychological formulations and zoological history need newer scholarship for updating.

- [Seth, God of Confusion.pdf](<../raw/Set/[H._Te_Velde]_Seth,_God_of_Confusion_A_Study_of_H(BookZZ.org).pdf>)

## C20 — Barney, Sobek article

**Status:** confessional/apologetic article containing an Egyptological digest.  
**Use:** useful evidence on Sobek, Amenemhat III, Fayum water management, titles, statues, and texts. Its inference from that evidence to Joseph Smith's Book of Abraham facsimile is not established by the Egyptology it cites.

- [2Barney_Sobek.pdf](<../raw/2Barney_Sobek.pdf>)

## C21 — Temple of Kom Ombo image

**Status:** modern photograph.  
**Use:** exterior/forecourt architectural reference for the paired Sobek–Haroeris temple. It does not itself document Set; ancient Ombos/Naqada, Set's center, is a different site.

- [Temple of Kom Ombo.jpg](<../raw/Set/Temple of Kom Ombo.jpg>)

## C22 — Sauneron, The Priests of Ancient Egypt

**Status:** scholarly monograph, Serge Sauneron.  
**Use:** priestly offices, training, temple life, knowledge, purity, ritual, and institutional setting. Its complete contents and targeted passages from all major divisions were reviewed. Older but foundational; Teeter supplies a newer practical synthesis, and Sauneron's claim that Egyptians were incapable of abstract thought is treated as dated rather than adopted.

- [The priests of ancient Egypt.pdf](<../raw/Set/Serge Sauneron The priests of ancient Egypt.pdf>)

## C23 — Pyramid Text selections

**Status:** primary translation selection.  
**Use:** offerings, ascent, divine identification, bodily renewal, enemies, and royal potency. Thirty-page scan required OCR.

- [Pyramid Texts.pdf](<../raw/March 22/Pyramid Texts.pdf>)

## C24 — Naydler, Shamanic Wisdom selection

**Status:** modern minority interpretation, Jeremy Naydler, 2005.  
**Use:** argues against exclusively funerary readings and for living royal mystical/initiation rites connected with the Sed festival. Valuable debate source, not established consensus. Nineteen-page scan required OCR.

- [Naydler Shamanic Wisdom.pdf](<../raw/March 22/Naydler Shamanic Wisdom.pdf>)

## C25 — Early Dynastic water-lily motif

**Status:** scholarly archaeological/botanical article, Pommerening, Marinova, and Hendrickx.  
**Use:** strongest project source for species identification, early imagery, faience vessels, chronology, and motif development. Its conclusions support antiquity/symbolism, not automatically intoxication.

- [Early Dynastic Origin of Water-lily motif.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Early Dynastic Origin of Water-lily motif.pdf>)

## C26 — Water lilies and lotuses in ancient Egypt

**Status:** illustrated popular/specialist article by Clair Ossian.  
**Use:** distinguishes Nymphaea from Nelumbo and surveys iconography, fragrance, offerings, Nefertem, sexuality, funerary renewal, and proposed narcotic use. Useful visual survey; psychoactive claims exceed the demonstrated evidence in places. Scan required OCR.

- [Water_lilies_and_lotuses_in_ancient_Egyp.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Water_lilies_and_lotuses_in_ancient_Egyp.pdf>)

## C27 — Nymphaea cults and pharmacology

**Status:** short medical-history article, Bertol et al., 2004.  
**Use:** proposes empirical pharmacological knowledge and compares Old/New World water-lily cults; discusses apomorphine. It is brief and hypothesis-driven, useful for leads rather than proof of Egyptian ritual dosage.

- [Nymphaea cults in ancient Egypt and the New World.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Nymphaea_cults_in_ancient_Egypt_and_the_New_World_.pdf>)

## C28 — Mandrake alkaloids

**Status:** scientific review/article on Mandragora chemistry.  
**Use:** supports presence and pharmacology of tropane alkaloids in mandrake. It does not establish a specific Egyptian ritual recipe or validate identification of every rounded fruit in art.

- [Mandrake Alkaloids.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Mandrake Alkaloids.pdf>)

## C29 — Entheomycological crowns

**Status:** highly speculative comparative paper.  
**Use:** reads crowns and religious imagery as mushrooms. Formal resemblance is not supported here by Egyptian text, residue, secure botanical context, or a demonstrated iconographic chain. Retained as an example of entheogenic interpretation, not as reliable Egyptology.

- [Entheomycological Crowns.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Entheomycological Crowns.pdf>)

## C30 — Archaeological evidence for Old World psychoactive plants

**Status:** broad scholarly review, Merlin.  
**Use:** surveys archaeobotanical evidence for opium, cannabis, ephedra, water lily, mandrake, and other taxa across the Old World. Useful comparative background; region, dating, identification, and ritual context must be checked plant by plant.

- [Archaeological Evidence for the Tradition of Psychoactive Plant Use in the Old World.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Archaeological Evidence for the Tradition of Psychoactive Plant Use in the Old World.pdf>)

## C31 — Sayin, psychoactive plants and religious rituals

**Status:** broad hypothesis-heavy review in article and later chapter forms.  
**Use:** catalogs psychoactives and altered-state claims across many cultures, then proposes that hallucinations helped generate religious imagery. The two files share much text but are not byte-identical; the 2016 chapter rearranges/revises the 2014 article. Its global generalizations and rhetoric are much stronger than its culture-specific evidence.

- [Psychoactive Plants Used during Religious Rituals.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Psychoactive Plants Used during Religious Rituals.pdf>)
- [The Consumption of Psychoactive Plants in Ancient Global and Anatolian.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/The Consumption of Psychoactive Plants in Ancient Global and Anatolian.pdf>)

## C32 — Sacred plants and the Gnostic Church

**Status:** speculative comparative article.  
**Use:** proposes cultural/temporal links among psychoactive plants, mystery cult, and Gnostic religion. The paper marks parts of its own argument as speculative. It is useful for reception questions, not direct evidence for Pharaonic practice.

- [Sacred Plants and the Gnostic Church.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Sacred Plants and the Gnostic Church.pdf>)

## C33 — Symbolism of plants in European-Mediterranean culture

**Status:** short scholarly botany/cultural-history article, Kandeler and Ullrich, 2009.  
**Use:** distinguishes Egyptian water lilies and Indian lotus, summarizes biology and solar/funerary symbolism, and mentions possible intoxicant use. The intoxication sentence is a secondary assertion, not a demonstrated ritual case.

- [Symoblism of plants- examples from European-Mediterranean culture.pdf](<../raw/Research Paper/Symoblism of plants- examples from European-Mediterranean culture.pdf>)

## C34 — Book of the Dead and Nuclear Physics

**Status:** pseudoscientific paper by John Frederick Sweeney.  
**Use:** claims funerary texts encode black-hole substratum, atomic creation, exceptional Lie algebras, octonions, sedenions, and a pre-Ice-Age global civilization. It relies on numerical/anachronistic resemblance and unsupported premises. Retain only for source criticism; do not use as Egyptology or physics.

- [The Egyptian Book of the Dead and Nuclear Physics.pdf](<../raw/Book of the Dead Report/The Egyptian Book of the Dead and Nuclear Physics.pdf>)

## C35 — Water-lily project images

**Status:** modern reproductions/crops of a New Kingdom tomb scene.  
**Use:** show mourners/ritualists with floral bouquets interpreted by the project as lily, mandrake, and poppy. The first is cropped and watermarked; the second preserves a much broader scene. Identification and pharmacological inference require independent iconographic support.

- [Mandrake Poppy Lily.jpg](<../raw/Research Paper/Mandrake Poppy Lily.jpg>)
- [better mandrake poppy lily.jpg](<../raw/Research Paper/better mandrake poppy lily.jpg>)

## C36 — Deity report outline assignment

**Status:** generic academic assignment guide.  
**Use:** explains how to structure a deity-report introduction, argument, evidence, references, and grading expectations. It contextualizes the Set drafts but contains no independent evidence about Egyptian religion.

- [Introduction Outline Assignment rev RJG 01 24 2017.pdf](<../raw/Set/Introduction Outline Assignment rev RJG 01 24 2017.pdf>)
