# Chronology

Dates before 664 BCE are approximate. Dynasties are a later organizing scheme, traditionally associated with Manetho, and boundaries can be arbitrary. The value of chronology here is to track changes in institutions and surviving evidence.

| Period | Approximate dates | Religious importance in this course |
|---|---:|---|
| Predynastic / Protodynastic | c. 5500–3100 BCE | Regional iconographies and cults develop; Upper and Lower Egypt move toward a unified state. |
| Early Dynastic, Dynasties 1–2 | c. 3100–2686 BCE | Kingship and state cult consolidate; early monumental and mortuary patterns take shape. |
| Old Kingdom, Dynasties 3–6/8 | c. 2686–2181/2125 BCE | Pyramid building; solar kingship; earliest surviving [[pyramid-texts]] in late Dynasty 5, initially for kings. |
| First Intermediate Period | c. 2181–2055 BCE | Political fragmentation and stronger local power centers; older royal mortuary resources spread and change. |
| Middle Kingdom, Dynasties 11–13/14 | c. 2055–1650 BCE | Reunification; rise of Amun at Thebes; [[coffin-texts]] on nonroyal coffins; major Sobek patronage in the Fayum. |
| Second Intermediate Period | c. 1650–1550 BCE | Hyksos rule in parts of Lower Egypt; later memory connects foreignness and Set in complicated ways. |
| New Kingdom, Dynasties 18–20 | c. 1550–1069 BCE | Imperial wealth and huge Theban temples; royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings; [[amduat-and-book-of-gates|Underworld Books]]; [[book-of-the-dead]]; intense documentation of ritual and personal piety. |
| Amarna episode, Dynasty 18 | c. 1350s–1330s BCE | Akhenaten elevates Aten, attacks Amun's name, relocates the court, and centralizes divine access through the royal family. See [[amarna-and-late-transformations]]. |
| Third Intermediate Period, Dynasties 21–25 | c. 1069–664 BCE | Political decentralization; elaborate coffins and mummification; animal cults; shifting foreign and Nubian rule. |
| Saite / Late Period, Dynasties 26–31 | 664–332 BCE | Archaism and renaissance; native and Persian rule alternate; some traditions intensify, and Set is increasingly vilified in many contexts. |
| Ptolemaic Period | 332–30 BCE | Greek-speaking rulers support Egyptian temples; great surviving temple programs at Edfu, Dendera, Kom Ombo, Esna, and Philae formulate learned local theologies. |
| Roman Period | 30 BCE–395 CE | Temple traditions and Demotic/Greek magical practice continue while Egyptian deities circulate across the Mediterranean; older institutions eventually contract. |

## Do not memorize this as a straight line of “progress”

Egyptian religious change was additive and recursive. Old language and imagery could be revived centuries later. A corpus might change audience or medium without disappearing. A local deity might become nationally prominent through royal patronage and later recede. Political conquest did not automatically erase temple traditions; Ptolemaic and Roman rulers could present themselves in pharaonic form.

## The funerary sequence

The instructor's chapter-one notes give a useful, deliberately simplified trajectory:

- Old Kingdom: royal ascent toward sky and stars;
- Middle Kingdom: a western destination and an Egyptian-like otherworld become more prominent;
- New Kingdom: mapped underworld journeys and Osirian judgment become especially visible.

This is a shift of emphasis, not three mutually exclusive doctrines. Solar, stellar, terrestrial, and underworld destinations overlap. See [[funerary-text-tradition]].
