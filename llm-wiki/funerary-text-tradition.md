# Funerary text tradition

The course's best metaphor is a roof of overlapping shingles. Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Underworld Books, and Books of the Dead inherit, reposition, and innovate; one does not simply replace the last.

!media[sethnakht-funerary-papyrus]

| Corpus | Main period of early prominence | Typical medium/location | Principal user | Recurring emphases |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[pyramid-texts]] | late Old Kingdom | carved inside royal pyramids | king, later some queens | ascent, stars, divine identification, offerings, bodily protection, royal power |
| [[coffin-texts]] | First Intermediate/Middle Kingdom | coffins and burial equipment | wider elite, not literally everyone | bodily integrity, food, movement, transformations, maps, Egyptian-like otherworld |
| [[amduat-and-book-of-gates|Underworld Books]] | New Kingdom | walls of royal tombs; later broader use | initially king with solar god | twelve-hour nocturnal cosmos, gates, divine populations, Re–Osiris renewal |
| [[book-of-the-dead]] | New Kingdom through Roman Period | papyrus, wrappings, coffins, tomb walls, objects | purchasers across a broader social elite | personalized spell collection, judgment, protection, transformation, Field of Reeds |

## Continuities

- identification with Osiris and other gods;
- restoration of body, mouth, heart, and senses;
- food, breath, clothing, and movement;
- knowledge of names and routes;
- defeating snakes, decapitation, fire, inversion, and hostile judgment;
- joining solar and Osirian cycles;
- speech and image functioning together.

## Changes of access are real but not simple “democratization”

Older scholarship described a democratization of the afterlife: royal privileges spread to nonroyal people. There is truth in the broader distribution of texts and Osirian identification, but “democratization” can overstate access. Elaborate coffins and papyri remained expensive; status, literacy, workshops, and institutional connections mattered. Royal and nonroyal traditions continued to differ.

## Text is not a fixed book

These corpora are selections and arrangements, not canonical books with one universal order. Ancient owners chose spells; workshops copied and adapted; titles and numbering are modern editorial tools. Images may carry content even when text is abbreviated or disappears in late copies.

UCL's corpus overview independently confirms this media-based continuity: Coffin Texts reuse Pyramid Text material, while many Coffin Text compositions enter later Book of the Dead traditions. See [[web-research-supplement#Funerary corpora overlap rather than replace one another]].

## Three models of the afterlife

The archive preserves sky ascent, a western/earthlike field, and a subterranean nocturnal journey. They accumulate rather than cancel. The deceased may be Osiris in a tomb, Re in motion, a star in the sky, a farmer in the Field of Reeds, and a ba visiting the living world.
