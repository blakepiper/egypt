# Deity field guide

Use iconography as an entry point, not an exhaustive identity. Crowns and heads can be shared; read inscriptions, companions, place, and action.

| Deity | Recognize | Relations and functions emphasized in the archive | Places |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amun** | tall double plumes; sometimes ram | hidden/transcendent power, air, creator, royal father; joins Re; triad with Mut and Khonsu | Thebes, Karnak, Luxor |
| **Amun-Re** | combined Amun/solar forms | national and imperial god; receives Opet renewal | Thebes |
| **Anubis** | black jackal or jackal-headed man | embalming, necropolis, guiding/protecting the deceased; embraces coffin at funeral; tests threshold knowledge | cemeteries; embalming tent |
| **Apep/Apophis** | giant serpent | recurrent non-order threatening Re's bark | Duat |
| **Aten** | solar disk with rays ending in hands | life-giving light; exclusive royal mediation under Akhenaten | Amarna; early East Karnak |
| **Atum** | anthropomorphic, double crown; solar/creator associations | self-generation, evening/completion, Heliopolitan Ennead | Heliopolis |
| **Banebdjedet** | ram or ram-headed | “ba of the lord of Djedet,” Mendes; appears in Plate 30's secret geography | Mendes |
| **Bes** | frontal dwarf, leonine features, feathered crown | apotropaic household protection, childbirth, sexuality, music | homes; birth contexts |
| **Geb** | reclining man, sometimes goose | earth; partner of Nut; parent generation of Osiris cycle | Heliopolitan theology |
| **Hapi** | corpulent male with pendulous breasts, aquatic plants | personified inundation and abundance; binds Two Lands | Nile scenes |
| **Hathor** | cow, cow-horn-and-disk crown, or woman | motherhood, sexuality, music, intoxication, joy, sky, west, welcome of dead | Dendera; Thebes |
| **Heka** | anthropomorphic; upraised-arms sign may be relevant in course iconography | effective power enabling divine and human ritual; linked with Sia and Hu | cosmic and ritual contexts |

| **Khonsu** | youthful sidelock and lunar disk/crescent; sometimes falcon | moon, travel/time, Theban triad; early ferocious imagery; late creator theology | Karnak/Thebes |

| **Mut** | woman with double crown or vulture form/headdress | mother/queen; Amun's consort; eye/solar leonine aspects; beer festival | Karnak/Thebes |
| **Nefertem** | lotus/water lily on head, sometimes lion associations | first fragrance/light from lotus; beauty and healing; Memphite triad | Memphis |

| **Nekhbet** | vulture, often hovering protectively | Upper Egypt; one of Two Ladies on royal crown | el-Kab / Upper Egypt |
| **Nephthys** | house-and-basket hieroglyph on head; winged mourner | sister/partner in mourning and protecting Osiris/dead | funerary scenes |
| **Nun** | male with water signs; paired with Nunet | primeval water and latent possibility | cosmogony |
| **Nut** | arched star-covered woman; sometimes cow | sky, mother of solar and Osirian gods; receives/rebirths sun and dead | coffin lids; tomb ceilings |
| **Osiris** | mummiform king, atef crown, crook and flail, green/black skin | dead king, regeneration, fertility, judge, ruler of westerners | Abydos, Busiris, funerary contexts |
| **Ptah** | mummiform man in close cap holding composite scepter | creation through heart/tongue, craftsmanship, Memphis; with Sekhmet and Nefertem | Memphis |
| **Re/Ra** | falcon-headed with sun disk or solar disk | visible sun, creator, king of gods, daily/nightly cycle | Heliopolis; everywhere solar |
| **Sekhmet** | lioness-headed woman with solar disk | destructive/protective Eye of Re, plague and healing; beer pacifies rage | Memphis; widespread |

| **Wepwawet** | upright jackal standard | “opener of ways,” royal/funerary processions, Sed festival | Asyut/Lycopolis; Abydos |

## Fast triads and groups

- Theban triad: Amun–Mut–Khonsu.
- Memphite triad: Ptah–Sekhmet–Nefertem.
- Osirian family: Osiris–Isis–Horus, with Set and Nephthys in the larger Ennead.
- Elephantine triad: Khnum–Satet–Anuket.
- Two Ladies: Nekhbet (Upper Egypt) and Wadjet (Lower Egypt).
- Four Sons of Horus: Imsety, Hapi, Duamutef, Qebehsenuef.
