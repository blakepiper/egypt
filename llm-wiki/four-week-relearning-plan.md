# Four-week learning plan

This is designed for roughly 30–45 minutes a day. If the trip is closer, treat each week as one intensive day.

## Week 1: Learn the grammar

1. Read [[start-here]].
2. Read [[chronology]] and sketch the periods from memory.
3. Read [[sacred-geography]]; practice the “upstream is south/Upper Egypt” reversal.
4. Read [[how-egyptian-religion-works]].
5. Read [[maat-isfet-and-kingship]] and [[heka-and-operative-ritual]].
6. Use [[glossary]] to test: maat, isfet, heka, Nun, Duat, akhet, ka, ba, akh.
7. Re-read the main synthesis in [[start-here]] without notes.

Checkpoint: explain why several creation stories can coexist and why ritual must repeat.

## Week 2: Meet the gods through relationships

1. Read [[creation-traditions]].
2. Read [[solar-cycle]].
3. Read [[osiris-isis-horus-and-set]].
4. Read [[deity-field-guide]] from functions to iconography, then backward from iconography to functions.
5. Read [[set]] and [[sobek]] as case studies in change, locality, and ambivalence.
6. Use [[visual-decoder]] with images in the source folder.
7. Use the creation and kingship checks in [[exam-recovery-guide]].

Checkpoint: identify ten gods by image and explain each through at least two relationships, not a one-word “domain.”

## Week 3: Practice religion

1. Read [[temples-priests-and-offerings]].
2. Read [[festivals-oracles-and-personal-piety]].
3. Read [[ptahhotep-and-ethical-life]].
4. Read [[death-funeral-and-the-dead]].
5. Read [[personhood-and-the-afterlife]].
6. Use the ritual and personhood checks in [[exam-recovery-guide]].
7. On an imagined temple visit, narrate movement from pylon to sanctuary and explain why access narrows.

Checkpoint: explain the daily offering ritual, priestly purity, three ways ordinary people approached gods, and why a tomb needed both burial chamber and chapel.

## Week 4: Read the afterlife and prepare for sites

1. Read [[funerary-text-tradition]].
2. Read [[pyramid-texts]] and [[coffin-texts]].
3. Read [[amduat-and-book-of-gates]].
4. Read [[book-of-the-dead]] and [[book-of-the-dead-plate-30]].
5. Read [[amarna-and-late-transformations]].
6. Read [[contested-interpretations]] and [[blue-water-lily-research]].
7. Review [[egypt-trip-field-guide]], [[visual-decoder]], and the funerary-literature checks in [[exam-recovery-guide]].

Checkpoint: compare four funerary corpora by date, location, intended user, imagined geography, and principal problems solved.

## Retrieval prompts for the flight

- Why is the west associated with death?
- What does “the king presents maat” imply politically and cosmically?
- Why does knowing a gatekeeper's name create passage?
- How can Set both murder Osiris and defend Re?
- Why are bread, beer, breath, movement, and a preserved name all afterlife issues?
- What changed at Amarna, and why did it fail to satisfy many traditional religious needs?
- What evidence would be required to prove ancient ritual ingestion of blue water lily?

## Optional direct reading

For one primary text per week, use the raw archive: Teaching of Ptahhotep; Contendings of Horus and Seth; Coffin Text selections; Plate 30/Chapter 125 materials. Read the wiki page first, then the translation, then amend your mental model with what surprises you.
