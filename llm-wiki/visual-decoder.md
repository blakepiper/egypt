# Visual decoder

!media[nauny-judgment]

## Read a scene in this order

1. **Orientation:** figures generally face toward the action, offering, shrine, or text they address.
2. **Scale:** larger size usually marks status, not physical distance.
3. **Head/crown:** generate candidates, not certainty.
4. **Object:** ankh, was, djed, sistrum, scroll, incense, offering, crook/flail.
5. **Gesture:** adoration, embrace, smiting, purification, nursing, protection.
6. **Caption/name:** hieroglyphs settle identities when iconography overlaps.
7. **Context:** temple wall, tomb, coffin, household object, papyrus, amulet.

## High-frequency signs

For the one-consonant phonetic repertoire inside the hieroglyphic system, read [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]]. Uniliterals are a narrow phonetic category, not proof that Egyptian writing was an exclusive alphabet; the visual and determinative functions in this decoder are part of why the mixed system endured.

| Sign/object | Meaning in context |
|---|---|
| ankh | life/breath given to nose or held as divine capacity |
| was scepter | dominion/power |
| djed pillar | stability; Osirian backbone/regeneration associations |
| tyet knot | Isis's protection, often red stone in funerary use |
| crook and flail | royal/Osirian authority |
| ostrich feather | Maat; truth/order/judgment |
| sistrum | music and pacification/celebration, especially Hathor; temple singer |
| incense cone / censers | purification and divine fragrance; “cones” remain debated |
| offering table | reciprocal nourishment and cult continuation |
| solar bark | movement of Re or processional divine presence |
| scarab | Khepri, becoming and renewal |
| uraeus cobra | Wadjet/protective royal fire |

## Crowns

- **White crown (hedjet):** Upper Egypt.
- **Red crown (deshret):** Lower Egypt.
- **Double crown (pschent):** unified Two Lands.
- **Atef:** Osirian white crown with side feathers.
- **Nemes:** striped royal headcloth, not itself a crown of Upper/Lower Egypt.
- **Cow horns + disk:** Hathor, Isis, or another goddess; use context.
- **Tall double plumes:** often Amun, Min, or related forms; use pose and crown base.

Some sources call a `wereret` crown the double crown, but terminology varies and crown names can be conflated. Use museum labels alongside the image itself.

## Priest cues

- leopard skin = sem priest;
- diagonal sash and/or scroll = lector priest;
- shaved head, clean linen = common purity ideal, not unique office;
- carrying shrine/bark = processional priestly team;
- king offering = ideological officiant; actual priest not necessarily pictured.

## Funerary scene cues

- black jackal at mummy = Anubis/embalming;
- two winged women = often Isis and Nephthys protecting Osiris/deceased;
- human-headed bird = ba;
- four small mummiform figures = Sons of Horus;
- scale + feather + heart = judgment;
- crocodile-lion-hippo composite = Ammit;
- fields/canals = Field of Reeds;
- small mummy-like worker with tools = shabti;
- red text = rubric/instruction or emphasis, depending on document.

## Presence has more than one form

An image can depict a deity, make a divine presence available, substitute for a person or offering, or belong to a ritual object. A living cult animal and a votive mummy are different kinds of evidence again. Do not collapse all of these into “symbolism” or “animal worship.” [[deity-field-guide]], [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]], and [[sobek]] apply the distinctions to images and remains. R023 and R050 provide the material-religion and animal-cult vocabulary.

## Materials carry social histories

Stone, pigment, wood, linen, metal, papyrus, and animal or human remains reached a scene through labor, exchange, patronage, and conservation. A quarry wall and a temple relief therefore belong to the same question about materials without having the same ritual use. [[monuments-labor-and-building-eternity]], [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]], and [[gebel-el-silsila-quarrying-sacred-landscape]] follow those connections. R014, R023, R066, and R068 support the material and labor frame.

## Museums and remains need ethical attention

Labels and display cases are interpretations, not transparent windows. Collection histories can include colonial extraction, unequal purchasing power, incomplete provenance, and changing institutional language. Human-remains dignity is a separate review from copyright or image licensing. Do not use a mummy as atmospheric decoration or infer consent from a public display. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]], [[legacy-of-ancient-egypt]], and [[death-funeral-and-the-dead]] provide the necessary context. R043, R044, R045, R056, and R057 support this practice.

For the J01 route, use the public site records and the textual transcript together. [[esna-khnum-temple-and-layered-town]], [[el-kab-nekheb-city-and-provincial-memory]], [[edfu-temple-town-and-sacred-history]], [[gebel-el-silsila-quarrying-sacred-landscape]], and [[kom-ombo-sobek-harwer-and-crocodiles]] keep a photographed detail tied to place, date, and later use.
