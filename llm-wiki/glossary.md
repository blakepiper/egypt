# Glossary

| Term | Concise definition |
|---|---|
| **akh** | effective, transfigured spirit capable of action among gods and dead |
| **akhet** | horizon or threshold of solar appearance/transformation |
| **Amduat** | “What is in the Duat,” a royal New Kingdom guide to Re's twelve-hour night journey |
| **amulet** | material form worn or placed for a specific protective or transformative power |
| **ankh** | hieroglyph and object signifying life |
| **apotropaic** | able to avert or repel harm |
| **ba** | mobile manifestation of a person, often a human-headed bird |
| **barque/bark** | sacred boat used by a deity in cosmic travel or festival procession |
| **canopic jars** | vessels associated with preserved viscera and the four Sons of Horus |
| **charter myth** | origin account that validates an institution or order |
| **cippus** | healing/protective stela, commonly Horus the Child mastering dangerous animals |
| **counterimage** | representation functioning as an effective substitute or presence |
| **Duat** | hidden realm of solar night journey, gods, and dead; not simply “hell” |
| **Ennead** | a grouping of nine deities, especially the Heliopolitan family from Atum |
| **etiological myth** | story explaining an origin, feature, or custom |
| **heka** | effective magical power and its divine personification |
| **hem-netjer** | “god's servant,” a high priestly title |
| **historiola** | short mythic precedent embedded in a spell |
| **ib/ab** | heart as seat of mind, memory, character, and testimony |
| **isfet** | disorder, falsehood, violence, and breakdown opposed to maat |
| **ka** | vital potency/continuing recipient or double sustained through offerings |
| **khaibit/shut** | shadow, a vulnerable extension or aspect of presence |
| **khery-heb / lector priest** | ritual specialist identified by sash and scroll, responsible for recitation |
| **Maat** | truth, justice, proper cosmic-social order; also its goddess |
| **natron** | naturally occurring salts used in purification and desiccation |
| **negative confession** | Chapter 125 declarations of wrongs the deceased says they did not commit |

| **Nun** | primeval undifferentiated water before and around creation |
| **Ogdoad** | group of eight primeval deities, especially at Hermopolis |

| **pylon** | monumental twin-towered gateway of a temple |

| **ren** | name as a constitutive element of identity |

| **shabti** | funerary figure intended to answer labor summons for the deceased |

| **tekenu** | enigmatic wrapped funerary bundle or figure in processions |

| **wab** | “pure” priest, often serving in temple rotation |
| **wabet** | “pure place,” embalming workshop or purification setting |
| **was scepter** | emblem of divine power/dominion |

## New terms for the expanded archive

| Term | Concise definition |
|---|---|
| **anicca** | Pali term for conditioned change or impermanence in early Buddhist teaching; it is not an Egyptian synonym for renewal. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **anatta** | Early Buddhist teaching on non-self; it should not be mapped directly onto Egyptian personhood terms. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **community-centered research** | Research that treats living communities as participants and historical subjects, not only as sources of data or scenery. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **cult animal** | An animal involved in a particular institutional or ritual relationship with a deity; the term does not cover every animal image or mummy. [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]] |
| **dukkha** | A term in early Buddhist analysis for suffering, stress, or unsatisfactoriness; it is not a diagnosis and not a translation of Egyptian misfortune. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **divine manifestation** | A specific form through which a deity becomes available or present in a ritual setting; it is not the same as a depiction. [[visual-decoder]] |
| **lived religion** | An approach that studies religion as people practice it in homes, workplaces, bodies, institutions, and relationships. [[households-work-and-unequal-access]] |
| **material religion** | Attention to the work of objects, substances, bodies, images, buildings, and sensory settings in religious practice. [[heka-and-operative-ritual]] |
| **Nubia** | A historical region with diverse communities and political traditions south of Egypt; it should not be treated as a passive extension of Egypt. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **provenance** | The documented history of an object, including its ownership, excavation, acquisition, movement, and display. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
| **reception** | Later interpretation, translation, appropriation, criticism, or religious use of an earlier tradition. [[legacy-of-ancient-egypt]] |
| **state formation** | The uneven process by which political authority, administration, extraction, settlement, and regional relationships become organized. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **transmission** | Evidence that a text, practice, image, or concept moved through a demonstrable chain of contact; resemblance alone is not transmission. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **votive mummy** | A deliberately prepared animal body offered in a particular religious economy; it is not identical to a living cult animal or a deity's image. [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]] |
| **theodicy** | An attempt to explain divine power or justice in relation to suffering, misfortune, and disorder. [[suffering-misfortune-and-divine-justice]] |

## Writing, transmission, and layered memory

| Term | Concise definition |
|---|---|
| **uniliteral** | An Egyptian sign conventionally used for one consonant; it belongs to a mixed writing system, not an exclusive alphabet. [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]] |
| **biliteral** | An Egyptian sign conventionally representing two consonants. [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]] |
| **triliteral** | An Egyptian sign conventionally representing three consonants. [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]] |
| **determinative** | A sign that clarifies a word's semantic category without normally adding its consonants. [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]] |
| **rebus principle** | Using a sign for the sound or consonants of the word it pictures rather than only for its object. [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]] |
| **acrophony** | Using a pictured sign for the first consonant of the word for that object in the writer's language. [[proto-sinaitic-and-the-alphabetic-breakthrough]] |
| **Proto-Sinaitic** | A modern label for a small and contested early alphabetic corpus associated especially with Sinai and Egyptian-contact contexts. [[proto-sinaitic-and-the-alphabetic-breakthrough]] |
| **consonantary** | A writing system whose normal signs represent consonants without routinely marking vowels. [[from-canaan-to-phoenician-greek-and-latin]] |
| **abjad** | A modern linguistic term for a consonant-focused alphabetic writing system; it should not erase the historical differences among Phoenician, Aramaic, Hebrew, and Arabic scripts. [[from-canaan-to-phoenician-greek-and-latin]] |
| **alphabet** | A writing system organized around signs for individual sound units; “Egyptian alphabet” is defensible only in the narrow uniliteral sense. [[uniliteral-signs-and-egyptian-phonetic-writing]] |
| **Ugaritic cuneiform** | A Late Bronze Age alphabetic cuneiform tradition that is a separate branch from the linear Phoenician consonantary. [[from-canaan-to-phoenician-greek-and-latin]] |
| **serekh** | A framed early royal name used in Egyptian display; its distribution is not automatically a map of administration. [[narmer-and-the-making-of-unification]] |
| **Dynasty 0** | A modern label for late Predynastic and Protodynastic rulers, not an ancient dynasty named in a surviving contemporary list. [[narmer-and-the-making-of-unification]] |
| **retainer sacrifice** | A debated interpretation of subsidiary burials near early royal tombs; the burial pattern and the proposed killing mechanism must remain separate. [[abydos-umm-el-qaab-and-the-first-writing]] |
| **garrison community** | A community connected to a military settlement or institution, without reducing all residents to soldiers or one uniform identity. [[elephantine-judaeans-and-egyptian-religious-life]] |
| **YHW / Yahu** | Forms of the divine name appearing in the Aramaic Elephantine documents. [[elephantine-judaeans-and-egyptian-religious-life]] |
| **parallelomania** | Treating isolated similarities as proof of borrowing without a dated route, intermediary, and patterned correspondence. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **literary influence** | A proposed relationship among texts that requires chronology, contact, correspondence, and a plausible mechanism rather than a shared topic alone. [[egyptian-wisdom-and-biblical-literature]] |
| **thirty sayings crux** | The debated relationship between a phrase in Proverbs 22:20 and the division of Amenemope into thirty sayings or chapters. [[egyptian-wisdom-and-biblical-literature]] |
| **moral accounting** | A broad comparative term for traditions that connect conduct, judgment, consequence, memory, or postmortem status; it does not prove a common origin. [[judgment-the-weighed-heart-and-later-afterlives]] |

## Expanded working vocabulary

| Term | Concise definition |
|---|---|
| **religion** | A modern analytical category for practices, relationships, institutions, materials, and claims about powers or worlds beyond ordinary control. It is not a single Egyptian essence. [[studying-religion-through-egypt]] |
| **ritual** | A patterned, materially situated action whose repetition, timing, authority, or effect matters to participants. [[ritual-uncertainty-and-continuity]] |
| **sensory religion** | Study of sound, smell, light, touch, taste, movement, and bodily perception as parts of religious practice. [[festivals-oracles-and-personal-piety]] |
| **religious authority** | The socially recognized power to interpret, perform, authorize, or regulate religious action. It can be distributed unevenly. [[temples-priests-and-offerings]] |
| **canon** | A later organizing label for texts treated as especially authoritative; ancient Egyptian corpora do not form one fixed canon in this sense. [[funerary-text-tradition]] |
| **orthodoxy** | A claim about correct teaching or practice. Use it cautiously where evidence shows local variation rather than a single enforcing institution. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **analogy** | A comparison that makes a question clearer without proving that two practices share an origin or meaning. [[studying-religion-through-egypt]] |
| **presentism** | Projecting present categories or imagined ancient practices onto a different historical setting. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **anachronism** | A person, category, object, or explanation placed in a period where it does not belong. [[chronology]] |
| **evidence** | A surviving trace used to make a bounded argument, with its material condition, context, and gaps kept visible. [[studying-religion-through-egypt]] |
| **reconstruction** | A reasoned account assembled from incomplete evidence, not a transparent return to the past. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **race** | A modern and historically changing system for classifying people; it must not be projected onto ancient populations as a natural ancient taxonomy. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
| **Predynastic** | The conventional label for periods before the First Dynasty, covering diverse regional communities and changing material traditions. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **Badarian** | An archaeological designation for a Predynastic cultural tradition in Upper Egypt; it does not name a uniform people or state. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **Maadi-Buto** | A label for connected but regionally varied Predynastic traditions in Lower Egypt and the Delta. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **Naqada** | A sequence of Predynastic cultural phases associated especially with Upper Egypt and later wider material convergence. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **civilization** | A broad, value-laden category for complex societies; it is a tool for comparison, not a prize awarded to one ancient people. [[egypt-and-mesopotamia-compared]] |
| **urbanism** | The study of settlement concentration, infrastructure, labor, institutions, and urban ways of organizing space. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **city-state** | A political formation centered on a city and its surrounding territory; it is not interchangeable with Egypt's territorial model. [[egypt-and-mesopotamia-compared]] |
| **territorial state** | A polity that claims authority across an extended and internally varied territory. [[egypt-and-mesopotamia-compared]] |
| **social stratification** | Unequal access to resources, status, work, authority, and visibility within a society. [[households-work-and-unequal-access]] |
| **cuneiform** | A wedge-shaped writing system used in Mesopotamia and elsewhere; its media and institutions differ from Egyptian scripts. [[egypt-and-mesopotamia-compared]] |
| **hieroglyphic** | A formal Egyptian script used especially for monumental and sacred contexts, though its functions changed across periods. [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]] |
| **hieratic** | A cursive Egyptian script used widely for administrative, religious, and literary writing on practical media. [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]] |
| **Demotic** | A later Egyptian script and language register used in administrative, legal, literary, and religious settings. [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]] |
| **scribe** | A person trained to produce or manage written records; the role included social power and different kinds of practical expertise. [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]] |
| **literacy** | The ability to use written systems, which can be partial, task-specific, and distributed through institutions rather than simply present or absent. [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]] |
| **numeracy** | Practical competence with counting, measurement, calculation, and quantity in a particular social setting. [[writing-knowledge-and-administration]] |
| **corvée** | Compulsory service or labor owed to an authority under specified historical arrangements; it should not be used as a synonym for every large workforce. [[monuments-labor-and-building-eternity]] |
| **redistribution** | The collection and reallocation of goods through an institution, estate, household, or authority. [[households-work-and-unequal-access]] |
| **household cult** | Religious activity organized in domestic spaces and relationships, including objects and practices that elite institutions record unevenly. [[households-work-and-unequal-access]] |
| **religious labor** | Work that creates, maintains, supplies, performs, records, or interprets religious practice. [[temples-priests-and-offerings]] |
| **disability** | A relation among bodily variation, social barriers, accommodations, and historical categories; it should not be inferred from an image alone. [[households-work-and-unequal-access]] |
| **uncertainty** | A condition in which outcomes, causes, intentions, or evidence remain unresolved. [[ritual-uncertainty-and-continuity]] |
| **ritualization** | The process by which action becomes marked as special through repetition, formality, timing, materials, or authority. [[ritual-uncertainty-and-continuity]] |
| **compulsion** | A felt or socially imposed pressure to act; shared ritual form does not by itself establish a clinical compulsion. [[ritual-uncertainty-and-continuity]] |
| **continuity** | Persistence of a relation, practice, name, institution, or material form through change, not proof of unchanged meaning. [[permanence-renewal-and-impermanence]] |
| **renewal** | A process that restores capacity, relation, status, or presence through repeated work. [[permanence-renewal-and-impermanence]] |
| **recurrence** | A return of a pattern or event that may repeat with variation rather than reproduce an original exactly. [[solar-cycle]] |
| **suffering** | Pain, loss, deprivation, fear, or distress as described or interpreted in a particular human setting. [[suffering-misfortune-and-divine-justice]] |
| **pain** | A bodily or affective experience whose description, cause, and meaning depend on historical and social context. [[illness-healing-and-protection]] |
| **divine justice** | A claim that divine power relates to moral order or human outcomes; Egyptian sources do not make one uniform argument about it. [[suffering-misfortune-and-divine-justice]] |
| **lament** | A voiced or written expression of grief, complaint, loss, or appeal. [[suffering-misfortune-and-divine-justice]] |
| **ritual failure** | The gap between an intended ritual effect and an outcome, including cases where sources record revision, ambiguity, or continued practice. [[ritual-uncertainty-and-continuity]] |
| **divination** | Practices that seek knowledge or guidance through signs, procedures, specialists, or consultation. [[festivals-oracles-and-personal-piety]] |
| **oracle** | A divinatory response attributed to a deity or sacred institution, often mediated through a procedure or official. [[festivals-oracles-and-personal-piety]] |
| **omen** | An event or sign interpreted as meaningful for a future or hidden condition. [[festivals-oracles-and-personal-piety]] |
| **fate** | A term for constraint or foreordained outcome whose meaning varies by text, period, and interpretive tradition. [[suffering-misfortune-and-divine-justice]] |
| **prophylaxis** | An action, object, or treatment intended to prevent harm before it occurs. [[illness-healing-and-protection]] |
| **remedy** | A material, verbal, dietary, or procedural response intended to address a bodily or situational problem. [[illness-healing-and-protection]] |
| **incantation** | Spoken or written words used as part of an intended effective procedure; the term does not imply irrationality. [[heka-and-operative-ritual]] |
| **healing statue** | A statue or stela associated with protective and healing texts, often involving Horus the Child and dangerous animals. [[illness-healing-and-protection]] |
| **incubation** | Seeking a divine or therapeutic response through sleeping or remaining in a sacred setting. [[illness-healing-and-protection]] |
| **retrospective diagnosis** | Applying a modern clinical label to historical evidence after the fact; it is especially unreliable when symptoms, context, and impairment are unknown. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **bioarchaeology** | The study of human remains and biological traces in archaeological context, with ethical and interpretive limits. [[death-funeral-and-the-dead]] |
| **nonhuman agency** | The capacity attributed to animals, objects, places, or other beings to affect relationships and action in a particular setting. [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]] |
| **manifestation** | A specific form or event through which a deity or power is understood to become present or available. [[visual-decoder]] |
| **embodiment** | The way a power, identity, or relationship is made present through a body, image, object, or practice. [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]] |
| **zooarchaeology** | The study of animal remains from archaeological contexts, including species, age, processing, use, and deposition. [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]] |
| **species identification** | The evidence-based assignment of remains or depictions to a taxon, with uncertainty where preservation or representation limits precision. [[animals-gods-and-nonhuman-agency]] |
| **monumentality** | The social, material, and political work through which buildings or objects acquire unusual scale, durability, visibility, or labor demands. [[monuments-labor-and-building-eternity]] |
| **pyramid complex** | A coordinated royal landscape of pyramid, temples, causeways, subsidiary structures, workers, and continuing mortuary service. [[pyramid-texts]] |
| **quarry** | A place where stone or other material is extracted, worked, marked, transported, and sometimes sacralized. [[gebel-el-silsila-quarrying-sacred-landscape]] |
| **work crew** | A historically situated group organized for a task, whose composition, status, skill, and conditions must be established rather than assumed. [[monuments-labor-and-building-eternity]] |
| **logistics** | The planning of people, materials, food, transport, timing, storage, and maintenance needed to make an undertaking possible. [[monuments-labor-and-building-eternity]] |
| **state capacity** | The historically variable ability of institutions to mobilize resources, information, labor, enforcement, and care. [[predynastic-egypt-and-state-formation]] |
| **experimental archaeology** | Controlled practical work used to test how ancient materials or techniques might behave; it does not recreate an ancient mind. [[monuments-labor-and-building-eternity]] |
| **pseudohistory** | A claim that imitates historical explanation while evading evidence, chronology, uncertainty, or scholarly correction. [[contested-interpretations]] |
| **dahabiya** | A shallow-draft Nile sailing vessel whose modern forms belong to particular histories of tourism, transport, and river management. [[nile-travel-dahabiyas-and-changing-river]] |
| **barrage** | A control structure that regulates river flow and water levels; it belongs to the managed modern Nile, not an undifferentiated ancient river. [[nile-travel-dahabiyas-and-changing-river]] |
| **lock** | A chamber system that raises or lowers vessels between water levels. [[nile-travel-dahabiyas-and-changing-river]] |
| **cataract** | A rocky stretch of the Nile that historically affected movement and political geography; the term does not mean an impassable waterfall. [[sacred-geography]] |
| **river corridor** | A connected zone of water, banks, settlements, fields, transport, labor, and ecological change. [[nile-travel-dahabiyas-and-changing-river]] |
| **Khnum** | A ram-associated creator god especially important at Esna and Elephantine, whose roles are local and historically variable. [[esna-khnum-temple-and-layered-town]] |
| **Nekhbet** | A vulture-associated goddess and patron of Nekheb and Upper Egyptian kingship. [[el-kab-nekheb-city-and-provincial-memory]] |
| **Horus of Behdet** | A local Horus form associated with Edfu; the label should not erase distinctions among different Horus traditions. [[edfu-temple-town-and-sacred-history]] |
| **Harwer** | The local form of Horus known as Horus the Elder at Kom Ombo, distinct from Horus son of Isis and Osiris. [[kom-ombo-sobek-harwer-and-crocodiles]] |
| **hypostyle hall** | A hall whose roof is supported by many columns, often creating a carefully staged sequence of light, access, and movement. [[temples-priests-and-offerings]] |
| **tell** | An archaeological mound formed through accumulated occupation and rebuilding; it is evidence of settlement, not simply a smaller temple. [[edfu-temple-town-and-sacred-history]] |
| **necropolis** | A cemetery or funerary landscape whose organization and use vary by period, place, and social group. [[death-funeral-and-the-dead]] |
| **speos** | A rock-cut sacred or commemorative space, such as the Speos of Horemheb at Gebel el-Silsila. [[gebel-el-silsila-quarrying-sacred-landscape]] |
| **quarry mark** | An inscription, sign, or trace cut or left during extraction, transport, building, or administration. [[gebel-el-silsila-quarrying-sacred-landscape]] |
| **paired sanctuary** | A temple complex organized around two divine axes and related ritual programs. [[kom-ombo-sobek-harwer-and-crocodiles]] |
| **participant consent** | Permission from a person who understands an activity and its possible recording, publication, audience, and consequences. [[living-nile-communities-work-food-and-hospitality]] |
| **reciprocity** | An exchange whose obligations, benefits, and terms are made intelligible and fair to the people involved. [[living-nile-communities-work-food-and-hospitality]] |
| **cultural tourism** | Travel organized partly around people, places, practices, heritage, or cultural interpretation, with uneven benefits and pressures. [[living-nile-communities-work-food-and-hospitality]] |
| **living heritage** | Present practices, places, skills, memories, and identities that people maintain, change, debate, or use now. [[living-nile-communities-work-food-and-hospitality]] |
| **mediation** | The work by guides, translators, organizers, researchers, and platforms that shapes what visitors encounter and understand. [[living-nile-communities-work-food-and-hospitality]] |
| **Kush** | A series of Nubian political formations centered in different places and periods, including Kerma, Napata, and Meroe. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **Kerma** | A major Nubian center and archaeological tradition whose history cannot be reduced to an Egyptian periphery. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **Napata** | A Nubian political and religious center associated with Kushite rule and the sacred landscape of Gebel Barkal. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **Dynasty 25** | The conventional label for Kushite rulers who controlled Egypt in the early first millennium BCE; it should not erase their Nubian political context. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **displacement** | The forced or pressured movement of people from homes, land, or community networks, including the effects of dam projects. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **salvage archaeology** | Archaeological work undertaken under pressure from construction or inundation, often preserving sites while leaving social costs underrepresented. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **Nubian languages** | A diverse set of languages and speech communities with their own histories, identities, and changing conditions. [[nubia-kush-displacement-and-living-identity]] |
| **Buddhism** | A diverse family of traditions shaped by teachings, practices, institutions, communities, and histories rather than one anti-ritual essence. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **early Buddhist discourses** | Textual sources used to study early Buddhist teaching, preserved through later traditions and not identical to every later Buddhist practice. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **Theravada** | A historically diverse Buddhist tradition and set of communities in which ritual, merit, monastic life, and lay practice all matter. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **aggregate** | One of the five skandhas in Buddhist analysis of experience; it is not a direct equivalent of an Egyptian personhood component. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **attachment** | Clinging or grasping in early Buddhist analysis; the term belongs to a specific account of suffering and practice. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **merit** | A beneficial ethical and ritual accumulation in Buddhist traditions, often connected to generosity, conduct, and dedication. [[egypt-and-early-buddhism]] |
| **adaptation** | A later use that changes inherited material to fit a new social, political, linguistic, or religious setting. [[legacy-of-ancient-egypt]] |
| **reinvention** | A deliberate or emergent creation of a past-facing form for present purposes, without requiring uninterrupted survival. [[egyptomania-and-popular-culture]] |
| **Egyptomania** | Modern fascination with and reuse of Egyptian forms, symbols, and stories, especially in European and North American popular culture. [[egyptomania-and-popular-culture]] |
| **Egyptian Revival** | A modern architectural and design style that draws on imagined or selectively interpreted Egyptian forms. [[egyptomania-and-popular-culture]] |
| **Hermetism** | A later religious and philosophical tradition built around texts attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, not a transparent survival of pharaonic religion. [[egyptian-religion-in-greek-and-roman-worlds]] |
| **esotericism** | A modern scholarly category for restricted, initiatory, or hidden-knowledge traditions; it should not be used as a synonym for all ancient ritual. [[egyptomania-and-popular-culture]] |
| **Orientalism** | A history of representing the East through unequal scholarly, artistic, and political frames. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
| **partage** | A colonial division of excavated finds that shaped ownership, museum collections, and the movement of Egyptian heritage. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
| **repatriation** | The return of a person, object, or cultural material to a community or country with a claim to it. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
| **decolonization** | Work that challenges colonial control over collections, categories, authority, access, and historical narration. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
| **Afrocentrism** | A range of approaches that center African histories and agency; individual claims still require evidence and should not replace one hierarchy with another. [[egypt-africa-and-modern-identity]] |
| **counter-memory** | A memory or historical account that contests an officially dominant narrative and makes excluded experience visible. [[egypt-africa-and-modern-identity]] |
| **biblical Egypt** | Egypt as represented in biblical texts and later interpretation, distinct from using those narratives as direct Egyptian historical evidence. [[egypt-in-biblical-and-christian-memory]] |
| **Exodus** | A biblical narrative of departure whose historical referent and relationship to Egyptian evidence remain debated. [[egypt-in-biblical-and-christian-memory]] |
| **cultural memory** | The ways communities select, transmit, reshape, and contest accounts of the past. [[legacy-of-ancient-egypt]] |
| **historicity** | The question of whether and how a narrative, person, event, or place can be situated in recoverable historical circumstances. [[egypt-in-biblical-and-christian-memory]] |
| **Kemeticism** | Modern religious and spiritual movements that draw on ancient Egyptian names, texts, deities, or symbols; they are neither automatically uninterrupted survivals nor mere fantasies. [[egypt-africa-and-modern-identity]] |
| **revival religion** | A modern religious movement that reconstructs, renews, or reworks an earlier tradition. [[egypt-africa-and-modern-identity]] |
| **digital religion** | Religious practice, identity, authority, and community shaped through digital media and networked spaces. [[egypt-africa-and-modern-identity]] |
| **human remains** | Human bodies or body parts retained, studied, displayed, or circulated as evidence; they require dignity review in addition to rights clearance. [[death-funeral-and-the-dead]] |
| **dignity review** | An ethical assessment of whether showing or describing human remains is necessary, contextualized, and respectful, independent of copyright or permission. [[egyptology-museums-and-colonialism]] |
