# Sobek

Sobek concentrates the ambivalence of the Nile crocodile: dangerous predator, master of water, sexual and vegetative potency, royal force, and protective god.

## Identity and iconography

Sobek appears as a crocodile or crocodile-headed man. Crowns can combine plumes, horns, a solar disk, and uraeus; the was scepter and ankh express dominion and life. A living sacred crocodile or crocodile mummy could serve cultic presence, but practice varied by place and period.

## Water, fertility, and kingship

The Pyramid Texts already identify the king with Sobek emerging from floodwater and greening vegetation. Coffin Texts call him lord of water and use his aggressive vitality in the deceased's transformation. Crocodile danger becomes divine protection when aligned with king or worshipper.

The course exam preserves a memorable myth: Sobek makes a fish trap or net to recover Horus's severed hands from the Nile. The story joins technological invention, bodily restoration, river knowledge, and the Horus–Set conflict.

## The Fayum and Amenemhat III

The archive's Sobek article assembles real Egyptological evidence for strong Middle Kingdom Sobek patronage in the Fayum, especially under Amenemhat III: water-management concerns, Crocodilopolis, royal building, names incorporating Sobek, statues, and later accounts of sacred crocodiles. Its archaeological synthesis is useful.

Its controlling purpose, however, is Latter-day Saint apologetics: it argues that Sobek validates Joseph Smith's label “idolatrous god of Pharaoh” in a Book of Abraham facsimile. Evidence that Sobek was important to Amenemhat III does not establish the facsimile's claimed Abrahamic interpretation. Keep the Egyptology separate from the apologetic inference. ## Kom Ombo

The Ptolemaic temple at Kom Ombo has paired cult axes for crocodile Sobek and falcon Haroeris (“Horus the Elder”), with associated divine families. Its symmetrical plan turns potentially opposed powers into a shared temple order. Crocodile mummies and images make Sobek's local presence vivid.
