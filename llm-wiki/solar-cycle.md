# Solar cycle

The sun is not a static object but a divine process of becoming, aging, dying, passing through hidden conditions, and being reborn. This cycle is the clearest daily demonstration that order requires recurrent transformation.

## Forms of the sun

- **Khepri:** “the one who comes into being,” scarab form associated with dawn and becoming.
- **Re/Ra:** solar power crossing the visible sky in a bark.
- **Atum:** completed or aged sun, especially at evening.
- **Re-Horakhty:** Re as Horus of the two horizons.
- **Ram-headed nocturnal sun:** a common Underworld Books form, sometimes described as Re's ba.

The forms overlap. They express phases and relations rather than separate celestial bodies.

## Day and night

During the day Re sails from east to west. At sunset he enters the [[sacred-geography|Duat]], where the [[amduat-and-book-of-gates]] divide the night into twelve hours. The solar bark passes gods, the dead, gates, caverns, bodies, waters, and fiery zones. The journey distributes light, breath, time, and renewal to inhabitants while also exposing Re to danger.

The chief opponent is **Apep/Apophis**, a serpent of non-order who attacks the bark and threatens to halt succession. Gods including Set restrain, spear, cut, or bind Apep. Set's role here prevents him from being reduced to the Egyptian “devil”: violent force properly placed can protect cosmic rhythm.

## Re and Osiris

At the night's depth, the mobile solar power encounters Osiris, the enduring regenerative body of the dead king. Their union renews both: Re receives the potency needed for rebirth, while Osiris receives solar animation. This is not a permanent merger; renewal depends on conjunction and separation. The morning sun emerges, often through the scarab or a birth image.

## The solar cycle as model

- **Creation:** every dawn repeats first light from Nun.
- **Kingship:** the king sustains Re's order and defeats Apep-like enemies.
- **Funerary hope:** the deceased joins Re's cycle, gains light and mobility, or becomes an akh.
- **Temple ritual:** awakening the cult image parallels sunrise and first manifestation.
- **Plants:** the blue water lily's opening/closing became a sign of rebirth; see [[blue-water-lily-research]].
- **Time:** cyclical **neheh** and enduring/completed **djet** are coordinated rather than opposed.

## Do not flatten the story

It is tempting to narrate “the Egyptians believed the sun died at night and was reborn.” The richer point is that monuments turn night into a differentiated ritual geography. Each hour has inhabitants, names, exchanges, and threats. Knowledge of this order lets king and deceased participate in it. The images do not simply illustrate a text; text and image together make the hidden cycle present in the tomb.
